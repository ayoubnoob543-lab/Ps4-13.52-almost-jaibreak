2179:To trigger the exFAThax v2 bug, it is required to make a exFAT header with a custom blsize (block size) and upcase table data_len (data length), such that the 64-bit signed integer inequality -blsize < data_len holds. In this case, since table_len = (data_len - 1) + blsize, table_len can become smaller than it should because of roundup overflow, and is passed to sceFatfsCreateHeapVl(). This function will therefore only allocate a small buffer compared to blsize and data_len, and as a result overflow and corrupt subsequent objects on the heap when calling UVFAT_ReadDevice().
2180-
2181:In the exFAThax v1 disclosure, TheFloW adviced to take blsize=0x200 and data_len=0x0000000100000200. Maybe consciously, TheFloW gave in his PoC a positive value for data_len, when considered as a signed 64-bit integer, so the bug concerned by exFAThax v2 was not triggered. In his minimal implementation of exFAThax v1, ChendoChap kept blsize=0x200 and took data_len=0x00FF000000001000, which is also positive. To trigger exFAThax v2 we have two possibilities. The first one is to take a negative data_len which is compensated by a positive blsize. We should take a valid blsize, for example 0x200, and data_len=-bl_size+slide where the slide must be between 1 and bl_size. For example, taking blsize=0x200 and data_len=-0x1FF, we have well -blsize = -0x200 < -0x1FF = data_len. The second method is to take a negative blsize and a positive data_len. This forces blsize to be at least 0x8000000000000000 so the sector size would have to be of the order of millions of terabytes. Whilst this USB storage capacity may be achieved, with a huge money and power cost, the required RAM disallows this method.
